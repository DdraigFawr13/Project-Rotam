## Pitch

A fantasy caravan journey for the phone: each caravan master's career is one great crossing of a continent. Your crew and gear are a deck of named people and beasts who scar, age and die. What you carry pushes the world's 3-4 great crises forward, and every mark a career leaves changes the road for the next master. Morgan's itch: "Some sense of adventure like I did playing the Oregon trail as a kid but with more depth and life to it and permanence."

## Who it's for

Morgan for now, maybe others some day. Played on a phone "from couch or commute or bed", in "middle length sessions to long sessions".

## Core loop or idea

1. Pick the next leg of the crossing on a painted map.
2. Resolve travel events and fights by playing crew and gear cards. Wounds and exhaustion clog the deck until you rest.
3. Arrive in a town: meet 1-2 visible needs with a few goods, rest, recruit.
4. Your deliveries push the crisis tracks (for example a dragon waking, a war spreading), shown as changes on the map.
5. The career ends when you arrive or the caravan breaks. Its marks (waystations, opened passes, graves, crisis state) change the next career's routes, costs and events.

## What it isn't

- In Morgan's words: not a "spreadsheet", not "too much reading", not "boring".
- Not a trading sim: no price tables; 3-5 goods in all.
- Not a free-running world simulation, and not a base-builder.
- No separate card-battle mode in the prototype: fights use the same card play.

## Concepts dropped

- **A Waymaker** (one crossing per career, marks on the map): merged into the chosen concept.
- **C The Caravan Is the Deck** (one card system for everything): merged into the chosen concept.
- **D Age of Roads** (a living high-fantasy world): merged into the chosen concept in cut-down form. Its free-running simulation was dropped for its scope and reading load; 3-4 scripted crisis tracks remain.
- **B Merchant House** (trade drives the world): cut by Morgan. Its trade-first loop risked the spreadsheet feel. Its idea "what you deliver changes the town" was kept.
- **E Hearth on Wheels** (the caravan as a growing home): parked by Morgan. Its base-building pulls against travel. Its ageing crew and elders who retire into patrons fit into the card crew.

## Kill criteria

- After one full crossing in the prototype, Morgan doesn't want to start another (chosen by Morgan in round 1).

## Design brief

Start `planning-interview` from this brief. Do not re-ask the open questions it already answers.

**Settled:**
- The concept above: A's crossing per career, plus C's deck of named crew, beasts and gear, plus D's 3-4 scripted crisis tracks pushed by deliveries.
- Fights are resolved with the same card play as events (Morgan agreed, round 1 finding 7).
- Lasting change is mechanical: every mark a career leaves must change what the next master plays against.
- Trade is light: no price tables, 1-2 visible needs per town, 3-5 goods, results shown on the map.
- Build order: one complete crossing first, playtested, before anything that carries to the next career. The feeling Morgan wants came from wear and tear within one run; carry-over across careers is untested.
- Scope: a personal prototype, playable end to end, not store-ready (level 2 of 5 in the first brief).

**Open for design, in this order:**
1. What makes each card choice on the road interesting. This is the moment-to-moment game, and no concept answered it.
2. Crew depth: the first brief's skills, traits, conditions and morale, plus scarring, ageing and death.
3. Crisis tracks: which 3-4 there are, how deliveries move them, and how the map shows them.
4. Setting and tone: middle or high fantasy.
5. What carries across careers: retired masters as patrons or rivals, legacy cards, unlocked regions, a chronicle shown without heavy text.
6. Saving and resuming mid-crossing; engine and platform; hard limits.

**Don't reopen without new evidence:** a separate battle mode (reopen only if prototype fights feel thin); a trade-first loop; a free-running world simulation; base-building.
